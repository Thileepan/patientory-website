# Patient-Authorized Longitudinal Health Data — Patientory

> One reusable identity. One digital consent. One complete health record. Patientory unifies clinical, claims, pharmacy, lab, wearable, and patient-generated data into a single longitudinal record — accessed only with the patient's explicit authorization. Authorization is granular and revocable: the patient decides who sees what, for what purpose, and for how long.

## The problem: disconnected data, limited insight, higher costs

Healthcare organizations struggle to access complete, accurate, up-to-date patient data — because consent is hard to obtain, fragmented, and not portable.

- **Difficult to obtain consent** — manual, paper-based, inconsistent consent processes result in low participation
- **Fragmented data** — clinical, claims, pharmacy, and social-determinant data sit siloed across providers and systems
- **Poor identity resolution** — inaccurate matching and duplicate records lead to incomplete profiles and costly errors
- **Slow, manual processes** — time-consuming outreach and record requests delay care and increase administrative burden
- **Limited compliance visibility** — no clear audit trail of consent or data access increases regulatory risk

The consequences: duplicate testing and avoidable utilization, inaccurate risk scoring, fragmented care and delayed interventions, low patient engagement, and regulatory exposure.

## The solution: one record, every care setting, a lifetime of context

A longitudinal health record follows the patient across every provider, plan, pharmacy, and device — not just a single visit or a single system. Patientory aggregates data from across the ecosystem, normalizes it to FHIR, de-duplicates and links it to one verified identity, and keeps it current in real time. Complete. Current. Consented. AI-ready.

## Patient Access Services — how it works

Patientory serves as the identity and consent layer between patients, providers, payors, and data networks:

1. **Patient logs in** — via the Patientory app, a partner app, or an AI assistant, and is prompted to verify identity.
2. **Identity verification** — completed with a trusted IA2-level identity provider such as ID.me or CLEAR; the app securely stores the resulting IA2 token.
3. **Network authentication** — CMS Aligned Networks authenticate the IA2 token and the requesting application, then query participating QHINs for matching patient records.
4. **Consented data return** — data providers authenticate the request and return matching records, filtered to remove anything the patient excluded in their consent.

Verify once, reuse everywhere: the patient's digital identity and consent travel with them across every app, provider, and plan.

## Five layers of trusted health data infrastructure

1. **Identity & Authentication** — verify member identity through CMS-aligned digital identity providers, link it to a unique Patientory ID, and maintain an identity graph across patients, providers, and payors.
2. **Consent Management** — capture granular consent by data type, purpose, duration, and entity, with versioning, revocation, and a complete immutable audit trail.
3. **Longitudinal Data Infrastructure** — aggregate, normalize (FHIR), de-duplicate, and link data from across the ecosystem into a unified, real-time record for every patient.
4. **AI-Ready Data Platform** — structured, normalized data that powers care management, population health, risk stratification, remote patient monitoring, and value-based care analytics.
5. **Secure Data Exchange** — standards-based exchange (FHIR & SMART on FHIR APIs, TEFCA/QHIN ready), with every request checked against active consent policies.
